We need more guidance for what happens after the veil opens.

People encounter oneness, undergo ego death, feel the presence of god, experience kundalini awakening, or enter the mysteries through entheogens—and then have to figure out how to integrate with what they have encountered.

Many people begin an awakening process then become destabilized because there is no mentorship available. We need elders, the mentors, and communities that can help us discern and integrate. Who teaches us how to carry revelation into a body, a relationship, a livelihood?

An opening can bring extraordinary awareness and sensitivity. It can also illuminate wounds and beliefs we do not yet understand. It's critical to build capacity to introspect, uncover, and integrate. 

These are some common distortions I wish I had guidance on in my journey.

## 1. The physical neglect trap

As subtle and spiritual worlds become more vivid, ordinary physical existence can start to feel burdensome. Why deal with dishes, money, maintenance, or the body when you can commune with the infinite?

That pull deserves attention. Sometimes what feels like transcendence contains an urge to escape the difficulty of being here.

The body belongs to the mystery too. So do food, sleep, shelter, movement, and the work of keeping things alive. A garden requires tending even after you recognize god in every leaf.

We live oneness through particular bodies, with particular needs and responsibilities. Integration asks us to bring what we discover into those conditions.

Feed the body that carries the revelation. Maintain what your life depends on.

## 2. The acceptance trap

An experience of unity can dissolve old judgments. You may begin to recognize the suffering beneath behavior you once condemned, or understand why someone inhabits a worldview that previously seemed incomprehensible.

But understanding a behavior does not establish the ethicality of it. Ultimately, all is one, but we also happen to exist in contingent reality. We experience physical limitation and can only do so much. At this layer of existence, ethics and wisdom matter. Compassion does not make every action equally wise.

You can both recognize someone’s divinity and how their behavior may hurt and still hold them accountable.

Love needs discernment. Otherwise, “everything is valid” becomes a way to abandon the people who need protection—including yourself.

## 3. The purification and destruction trap

Once you begin seeing exploitation and suffering in existing systems, you may feel inclined to rebel against, reject, or even destroy the systems causing that pain. That impulse can extend to everything associated with them: money, institutions, medicine, law, commerce, civilization itself.

Grief and anger is warranted. But honoring every experience does not make every interpretation true, or every response equally effective. If we desire less suffering, we need to examine whether our actions actually move us toward it.

Rejection can bind us to what we oppose. When our identity becomes organized around fighting an enemy, we risk reproducing its patterns: domination in the name of liberation, exclusion in the name of purity, coercion in the name of love. Within oneness, the thing we want to cast out still belongs to the whole. In daily life, we are entangled with it through habits, dependencies, relationships, and wounds. Declaring ourselves separate does not dissolve those connections.

Become curious about the “bad” thing. Try to understand it from within: what needs sustain it, what pressures shape the people inside it, and where its workings contradict its stated purpose. That understanding can reveal what a successor must preserve, what it must change, and how it might transcend the broken system without recreating its harms.

Money, for example, can be understood as a shared language for exchanging human energy, labor, and resources. Scrutinize the particular dynamics of debt, interest, derivatives, or inflation rather than treating exchange itself as the source of harm. What arrangements extract value, concentrate power, or shift costs onto others? What would allow people to coordinate and exchange more fairly? An alternative still needs collectively compatible ways to meet those needs.

The same discernment applies to healthcare, law, and other inherited institutions. Abruptly rejecting their support can add unnecessary suffering before an alternative is ready. Use what sustains you while developing greater self-sufficiency and reciprocal support. Let the freedom you gain transmute into capacity to build new collective systems. Interdependence remains part of the work.

Sometimes resistance, refusal, and dismantling are necessary. Let them serve a transition that people can actually live through. If the urge to “burn it all down” is overwhelming, make room to grieve the very real pain of living within exploitative systems. Grief deserves care; it need not carry the entire burden of designing what comes next.

Bring that energy into building something that can sustain the life you want to make possible.

## 4. The deity and authority trap

You may encounter gods, ancestors, spirits, guides, extraterrestrial beings, or other intelligences. Their knowledge, power, or capabilities can make it tempting to assume they also know what is best for you.

Keep your discernment. Greater knowledge or more advanced technology does not establish an alignment of agendas. Ask what the entity desires, what you desire, and what your exchange creates together.

An agenda is a direction of desire. Different aims can be compatible without being identical, and an exchange can be genuinely symbiotic, nourishing everyone involved. Within oneness, relationship is how the whole discovers and develops itself. Discernment asks whether this particular relationship supports the experience and growth you actually desire.

A guide might delight in opening new doors while you desire help integrating what is already open. Neither desire needs to be wrong for the timing or exchange to be mismatched. More revelation is not always what you are asking for.

The same discernment applies to teachers, healers, channelers, communities, and even your own internal parts. What are we each inviting into this exchange? What do we want to cultivate together? What pace, boundaries, and forms of participation support that? If you do not know what you desire, it becomes easier to enter an experience you did not anticipate. Take time to discover and express your desires, including where you are still unsure.

Respect for boundaries is an expression of love. When fear arises, there should be room to pause, find reassurance, and choose whether to continue. A request to stop deserves respect. The trickster has a place in the mysteries too: light-hearted mischief can loosen certainty and invite growth. But the promise of growth does not override your right to stop; you can welcome surprise without consenting to every form it takes.

You do not need to settle the ultimate nature of an encounter before deciding whether to follow its guidance. When a source’s purposes are unclear, leave room for uncertainty rather than assuming either benevolence or hostility. Let trust grow with evidence of compatibility, reciprocity, and respect for your agency.

There should always be room to update the agreement or opt out. Changing course can be challenging, especially when attachment, habit, or obligation has formed. That difficulty does not make an agreement irrevocable. Your participation can change as your desires and understanding change.

## 5. The revelation and certainty trap

An information download can arrive as images, sensations, symbols, or a knowing that has not yet found words. Give yourself room to receive it before deciding exactly what it means.

The mind often rushes to fit new information into something familiar. Unconscious beliefs, fears, and desires can supply a story so quickly that we mistake our interpretation for part of the original download. Holding the information loosely helps us notice what we received and what we subsequently added.

Remote-viewing training has a useful term for this: analytical overlay; where interpretation, memory, and imagination intrude on the impressions being recorded. The practical discipline is to record the impressions, if possible, before rushing to identify them.

That said, sometimes you just *know*. Sometimes it's so clear. The juxtaposition between before and now can be immense. Maybe the contrast between the knowing and mundanity can make bring up doubts. If that's the case, it's okay to remind yourself of the magic.

Too much certainty can close possibility. Leave room for mystery. If you learn to love the unknown, it will bring you the wildest gifts. Balance here between the extremes of doubt and conviction is important.

## 6. The manifestation trap

I approach manifestation as a practice with specific steps. Bashar’s [seven steps of manifestation](https://www.bashar.org/sevensteps) move through vision, desire, belief, acceptance, intent, action, and allowance:
1. envision what you desire
2. feel your excitement for it
3. believe in its possibility
4. accept your capacity to participate
5. form the intention
6. embody it in action
7. allow the outcome to unfold

One trap is doing the internal work while leaving physical action out of the practice. If you desire new encounters but never vary where you go or what you do, you narrow the probability space. Go somewhere new. Follow an invitation. Begin a conversation. Action opens more opportunity.

Manifestation skill, beliefs, and physical participation work together. Leave room for extraordinary synchronicity, including outcomes you could never have planned. You can also give that synchronicity more openings through which to reach you. Taking action is part of steering your reality.

Another trap is turning the lag between intention and manifestation into a judgment about yourself. An outcome need not arrive immediately. A delay can become a loop: “It hasn’t happened, so something must be wrong with my consciousness,” followed by more self-monitoring, more pressure, and more judgment. Let the interval remain open.

It may be helpful to view co-creation as a marketplace of possibilities. Your intention places a bid whose weight comes from your willpower, clarity of desire, and freedom from attachment. In this model, the bid automatically enters the network of all consciousness in reality, where compatible desires and possibilities meet and find acceptance. The exchange can involve many beings at once, with one unfolding answering several intentions in different ways.

I find a useful analogy in [polycomputing](https://www.mdpi.com/2313-7673/8/1/110) (Bongard & Levin): the same physical substrate can perform multiple computations simultaneously, with different observers drawing different useful results from it. I carry that image into co-creation: one event can participate in many journeys, serve several purposes, and hold different meanings for everyone involved.

Manifestation can unfold through a chain of synchronicities, each opening the conditions for the next. Keep participating, allow your intention to become clearer, and leave the route open.

## 7. The sensitivity trap

As your sensitivity to subtle energies deepens, certain environments, relationships, and forms of stimulation may become harder to tolerate.

Negativity, violence, or manipulation may cause more discomfort than before. 

Increased sensitivity can be a gift, but it is not without tradeoffs. Sensitivity can become a prison if the whole world becomes too painful to inhabit.

There at least two ways to keep your sensitivity and still engage with other. Discernment and Capacity.
- Discernment helps you choose what you allow close. Understand your desires, and limits, understand when they are not being met and become quick to act.
- Capacity is the way to reduce the desire to isolate. Capacity is the ability to remain present around distortion. Sometimes external friction can be an indicator to go within. If a wave if passing through another wave, it only is affected if there is sufficient distortion. But being affected doesn't automatically prove an inner distortion. Sometimes an old wound is being touched; sometimes the present situation is harmful. Often there's something to learn about both.

Leave when you need to. Rest when you need to. Explore what an experience reveals without making yourself responsible for everything that hurts you.

## 8. The spiritual bypassing trap

“Everything happens for a reason.”

“We are all one.”

“The soul chose this.”

Any of these can express a sincere spiritual understanding. They can also close a conversation precisely where grief, anger, or accountability needs room.

You can believe in a soul’s journey and still mourn what happened. You can recognize the divine in someone and demand that they stop hurting people. Forgiveness does not require renewed access.

Let your cosmology make room for the human experience.

Some things need tears, repair, restitution, or a clear refusal. Explaining them from eternity does not complete that work.

## 9. The spiritual identity trap

An experience of ego death can become the foundation of a new identity: the awakened one, the healer, the chosen messenger, the person who sees what everyone else cannot.

A calling can become entangled with the need to be exceptional. Disagreement starts to look like proof that others are asleep. Ordinary feedback becomes something you believe you have outgrown.

Watch what happens when your spiritual identity is questioned. Can you remain curious? Can you apologize? Can you learn from someone who does not share your vocabulary?

Inherent divinity belongs to the person who disagrees with us too.

A calling carries responsibilities. It does not confer exemption from them.

## 10. The peak experience trap

A profound ceremony or mystical opening can become the standard against which ordinary life feels inadequate.

Then comes the pursuit of another breakthrough: another ceremony, another initiation, another dissolution, another revelation powerful enough to restore the sense of arrival.

It helps to ask what the last opening has changed in your life. What have you practiced? What relationship needs attention? What unfinished task keeps waiting while you seek another answer?

Leave room between revelations. There may or may not be a rush.

In story, the return is important. There is a reason myths follow the traveler home: eventually the gift has to become something the village can use.

## 11. The creator/cog trap

You may swing between two extremes.

In one, you are the creator of everything, responsible for every circumstance through your consciousness. In the other, you are a helpless cog in systems too vast to influence.

Both obscure the scale at which you can act.

You participate in creation alongside other beings, histories, institutions, and material conditions. Your choices matter within that web, though they do not command every outcome.

Ask what is yours to change, what requires cooperation, and what you must presently live with.

This makes responsibility more precise. You can stop carrying the whole cosmos long enough to do your part in it.

## 12. The savior trap

A calling to serve can become an obligation to awaken everyone, heal everyone, or rescue people who have not asked for your help.

You may offer insight faster than someone can receive it, interpret their resistance as spiritual blindness, or exhaust yourself trying to complete another person’s journey.

Service requires consent. People have their own timing, language, relationships, and ways of finding meaning.

A good principle is to only offer what is overflowing in abundance for you. Listen before interpreting. Allow others to decline.

If you believe that you need to save the world you may also be unconsciously reducing others' agency. Perhaps they can save themselves. What would you do if you found out the world was allready saved? Usually doing that thing can bring more love to others than if you try to save it.

## 13. The arrival trap

Finally, there is the belief that awakening should end confusion, conflict, grief, or the need for help.

Then ordinary difficulty becomes evidence of spiritual failure. You conceal what you are struggling with because it threatens the story of who you have become.

Leave room to remain human.

Sometimes people can say they've become awakened, they've done the work. But largely as you become more sensitive smaller and smaller things become apparent. Sure, there can be phase transitions and major realizations. Pain may become less intense. Play may (and should) increase! But the path is an upward spiral.

You can encounter god and still need guidance. You can experience oneness and still have an argument to repair. You can undergo a profound initiation and spend years learning how to embody what it showed you.

## The End?

As more people explore and deepen their conscious awareness, I want guidance like this to be easier to find. These experiences are ordinary parts of human life that often go unspoken. We need places where we can speak openly about them, compare experiences, ask questions, and help each other integrate.

When these experiences go unspoken, someone can encounter them without ever having heard others describe something similar. It can be frightening and lonely without language, guidance, or someone to talk to. There needs to be room to explore the spiritual meaning of an experience while also caring for practical needs. Our explanations of reality should remain open enough to learn from the people living through it.

I hope we can make these conversations ordinary enough that people know where to turn. Somewhere you can say, “Something happened, and I don’t know how to understand it,” and find people willing to listen, share what has helped them, and explore with you.

We have a lot to discover. Despite the modern scientific rhetoric, science is not over. We are in the middle of history. I’d love for us to discover more of reality together.
